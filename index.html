          How to read your water meter

          1. Find it. Usually in the basement or utility room, near where the water pipe comes into your home.
          2. Check the unit. Look for m³ (cubic metres) or ft³ / CU FT (cubic feet) printed on the face, and pick it in the Unit box.
          3. Read the numbers left to right. The black wheels are whole units.
          4. Red or smaller digits come after the decimal point. Example: black 01234 + red 567 → type 1234.567.
          5. Skip the little spinning triangle or star — that's the leak indicator, not part of the number.
          6. Press Save reading. The time is filled in for you — change it if you read the meter earlier. Or tap 📷 to fill the number from a photo, then check it.
          7. Same time every day (for example after breakfast) gives the most accurate daily number.

          💧 Leak check: don't use any water for 30 minutes. If the red numbers or the leak indicator move, you may have a leak — a running toilet is the most common cause.

          🔒 Photo privacy: your photo is shrunk on your device, sent once to read the number, then thrown away. It is never saved — not on this device, not on our server.

          Meters differ — some show cubic metres, some cubic feet, and the number of digits varies. DropWise converts every reading to litres (1 m³ = 1,000 L, 1 ft³ ≈ 28.3 L). The drawing is an example, not a specific meter model.

          Buttons and the shower timer give estimates (City of Calgary figures). The water meter gives the measured total. Estimates are never added on top of the meter — the meter already includes everything.

          Stay healthy & calm

          Save the water you can — never the water your health needs.

          💧 Essentials first — never cut these

          • 🥤 Drinking water — drink whenever you're thirsty, more on hot or active days
          • 🍲 Cooking and washing food
          • 🧼 Handwashing and brushing teeth
          • 💊 Medical needs, babies, people who are sick, and pets

          Why 50 L? The World Health Organization says about 50–100 litres per person per day covers basic needs with few health concerns. DropWise warns you if your home goes below that.

                      🏘️ Check on a neighbour

                      Not everyone sees city alerts. Seniors, newcomers and neighbours who don't read English may not know the rules — or may be skipping water they need. A quick knock or text helps.

                      • 👋 Ask if they know about Stage 4 and if they need anything.
                      • 🖨️ Leave a large-print card with the rules (in the language chosen at the top).
                      • 💧 Remind them: drinking, cooking and hygiene water are never the thing to cut.
